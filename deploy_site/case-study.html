Content Refresh Opportunity Model

This model ranks existing pages by urgency for refresh review. It uses search and engagement signals to surface pages that are declining in performance but still have enough demand to justify editorial attention.

Model evidence

The final recommendation queue was built from 30,000 scored rows. A client-holdout validation split shows the selected random forest model improved Precision@50 from 0.240 to 0.740 versus the baseline rule.

What the queue shows

The top-ranked items are not just low-performing pages. They combine declining demand signals with evidence that the page still receives impressions, clicks, or sessions. That makes the recommendations usable for manual review, not automated publishing.

The queue is intentionally layered by confidence: high, medium, and low. That gives a practical human review workflow before any refresh action is taken.

Why the model works

The strongest signals are not just page count or traffic. They include duration with impressions, average position, content age, and CTR behavior. That helps the model identify pages that are declining but still important enough to refresh.

  • Impressions and sessions show demand exists.
  • Average position and CTR capture search performance weakness.
  • Page age and content length help avoid chasing too-fresh or too-short content.

Validation and model trust

The model was selected with a client-holdout split, meaning the validation set keeps the same clients out of training. That is the safest way to avoid recommending pages that only look good because the model memorized one client’s behavior.

Manual review remains part of the workflow: the queue points reviewers to likely refresh opportunities, but editorial judgment still decides what gets updated.
